Ghazal for the Rot

the flowers on my windowsill grow parched. the hours stumble forward. like the legs of a wounded deer before becoming carrion.

like a crow, my birdlike intuition senses things nearing its end. the loose translation of your mouth sharpening into carrion.

I had hungered for that mouth once. less crow, more human. that month, my lover left home. took nothing but a carry-on.

I keep losing you in cluttered rooms, in a jewelry box or a tin of altoids. strange how even metal animates into carrion.

a bear-trap catches an elk's hooves in the brush. the sun's glint from my necklace blinds you. so easy to mistake the flesh of something breathing as carrion.

I am guilty of leading you astray. my heart some tormenting magnet. underneath a pinprick sky, we had spoke of memories as the voices of ancient hydracarians,

swimming around in the gross ephemeral snapshot of primordial soup. a still-life with maggots. the botfly's last warmth, in the rotting flesh of carrion.

in the last month we dated, I only woke to bed rot and eat. I followed all your tracks in the dark. you told me that it's instinct: we carry on

living. even as your grandfather's body turned to mulch beneath the tan leather of your boot. even as your sad gaze turned upwards toward the araucarian

canopy, sorrow the giant megafauna stumbling in the jurassic. dropping myself from taller trees, circling the cliffside. sleeping with men out of nostalgia. my lust Icarian.

but you know where all roads lead. you know where the headlights find their end. the flowers you gifted to tire-treaded carrion,

wilted and dying, returning to the plush city of my body. you have to understand that in those days, my head was just carrion.

so I guess sorry that I couldn't love you the way that you wanted. sorry that I missed all seventy-eight of your calls. for months I couldn't hope to carry on.

but before that, on the lake, the lights had narrowed into slits. cars orbit its refracted body. love, I see the crows circle overhead. they smell carrion.

it's november and nothing has ended yet. we make-out in your car and the steel creaks as if living. the sun still rises. and if I speak of carrion

it's only to spare you the voices of the dead. and when I warn you, you listen, no wounded deer in the bramble. only one starved crow. its brutal heart, just carrion.
