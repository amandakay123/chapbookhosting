sister

in you I see our father and the shyness of breath, too scared of exhale. for years our family has carried shame in the small of our backs. for years I watched the persimmon trees in the back yard wilt over the weight of their own fruit, swollen and rotting in the sun. what do we take from the earth but lost loves? years and oceans turn rainwater in your grasp. imagine a worm, burrowing into the dirt. it swallows its own tail. by which I mean yes, we take but we give so much to the earth and what else am I left with? a lifetime of sacrifices. investments go unreturned. I wonder if, in time, we will huddle in the same empty space, dew retracing the rotting bodies of our roots.
