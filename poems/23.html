Love Poems in the Wasteland

After Hozier's Wasteland, Baby! and T.S. Eliot's "The Waste Land"

I.

"April is the cruellest month"1 — T.S. Eliot

Mid-spring and the sun, crimson, lurching towards its own mouth. And even the birds have stopped screaming murder, bodies dirtied with spit. What you'll do next, in this time of crisis. Meditating on time. Croc stilettos. Gaze turned towards organic produce. Nara Smith. Things that are unafraid. Love as this country's last stand against everything that burns. As sad as it is, most good things end up killing me, like donuts or cheap wine. They start my days off sweet, heart following my gulps down. Here's the low thrum sob emerging between the earth's trembles or a similar catastrophe. From tragedy to tragedy: guppies in a fish tank, belly up, no longer dream about the sky—fireworks or gunshots, some unordinary flower. What does this foreign city know about your blood? Besides the clanking of metal and the urge to find better roofs to die on. Today, all my poems are full, by which I mean love from the wasteland is love without witness, a policeman's car stripped of sirens. Baby, the moon still halos overhead, even though the oceans have left only salt. I'm sleeping with the lights on, viewing everything small and dark approaching. In the face of death, no words are consequential, just ways to define what's missing. Love, the tea has gone cold. Saplings bury themselves in anticipation of warmer days. I'm searching for exit signs in all fatal conditions, highway bathrooms, us, taking in life by the spoonful, moving under this fishbowl sky. It doesn't matter if the love is dead, only that it was here. Our bodies just dogshit in the grass. All around me, music with closing credits. To my muse in torn Levi's, what a joy it has been to know you.

III.

"This music crept by me upon the waters" — T.S. Eliot

when I stop / to listen / to the street corner busker / on my way / to work / singing the sixties / catching my grandfather's face / in a storefront window / hearing / an echo / same laughter / that collapses / as much as it opens / a muscle / stripped / of blood / to be kind first be memorable / first remember / to do the day / justice / despite the melody / full of moss / and green tea stars / a rabbit-chased moon / with jujube eyes / to be music there first came / silence / a musician's body / backlit / against the city / and its haunts Mandopop / in a Honda Accord / to miss the sun / cosplaying as moon / her twin alien body / something sadder / all skies / like tall roofs / my bedroom everything heaving / at once / life distilled / to its smallest / names / o' atlas / o'calypso / I'm seeing faces / at every stop light / I mean / everything I see / must be mine to hold / almost / to close / distance / grasping a flower's reflection / in a koi pond / I mean / almost to me in the kitchen / I have not moved you / or your cups / or touched / the radio dial / to again hear static / voicemails / your hands were / a harmony of soil / my mother / says she's alright / as if to say / grandfather / grandfather / all around us / are clocks / almost in tune / to the blue night rhythm / sometimes / this song / is all / left / of you.3

II.

"HURRY UP PLEASE ITS TIME" — T.S. Eliot

To whittle away the chunks like a mid-century sculptor. Ages ago I singed my hair short, exchanging scissors for fireflies. It's been long enough since your last love that you've forgotten how to touch. Fire away—my country, as seen on TV. My father, the last great warrior. The worst part about hurting is when it doesn't—phantom limb with marble accents. My country, fifteen hours away, turned to chalk. Love, there's so much I can tell you about past lives, tinged with smoke. In the absence of memory, every childhood, even good ones, fell softly. Me with the blunt bangs, where all unfinished sentences burn, even I4

V.

"These fragments I have shored against my ruins" — T.S. Eliot

Any person who's loved knows how to end it. And I confessed to my first love on prime-time TV. I'd let the quiet wastelands of our bodies overlap, be watched by our neighbors in this country. Here's the billboards we leave in the rearview, immediate in its asking. I still want you to rub my back. Forgiveness as in becoming telephone static despite the closure. In confidence, I'll tell you this: you're my Hinge-date Eurydice. My best pick-up lines go unwasted, hotter in Tennessee. Imagine this: the reporter says there's a high chance of rain. It being that I loved you, I wanted to smother you. I loved you so I turned around, your back casting shadows by the minute. I cannot help that this leaving ruins me.5

XX.

For A,
A Cento

It's the hearing of a human spirit ringing. Feeding a little life with dried tubers, "Stella by Starlight," whisper music on those strings. Each day, you'd rise with me. Here is no water but only rock. Oh, let there be hotel complaints. The stench of the sea and the absence of green. Of thunder of spring over distant mountains. Your arms full, and your hair wet. Make your good love known to me or just tell me about your day. Run softly until I end my song. Dayadhvam: I have heard the key. As the shrike to your sharp and glorious thorn.6
