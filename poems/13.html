My Dear Crow

came billowing as dark and as strict as a stormcloud, carrying in his beak a collection of knickknacks: a button tied loosely with thread, the fur of an ermine torn from a rich lady's coat, a silver dollar engraved with the year of my father's birth.

back then his heart was as open as a window. collisions, now a leading cause of bird mortality. before glass, there was sand shifting beneath our feet and before that, the quagmire above which the graves of loved ones reside. soon, you'll learn that you are not defined by the air but the drag weighing upon you. bringing your coverts to your chest. stones home to sea. sap towards sky.

that night I thought of dying you came to the window. so loud a thud it was the sharp rise of lightning. corvus, bring me a string of my father's baby teeth instead of a blade. each time I wake the moon silvers into slits. that night we met, had the lights been on in my room, would you have flown off without saying hi? able to discern between the sky and the hollow just like it. or pecked at the window, demanding a tuft of my hair to drape over your ugly children. enshrouding them in the warmth of some larger creature, if not a god. you, a reminder that love becomes cloaked in the dark. were you watching me earlier then, above the overpass, where asphalt turns to red ochre?

30 feet above the ground for a fledgling's first flight, away from the sticks and the warmth of a rabbit's pelt. that spring day, I felt the heat that warms the shores of distant beaches and the heat that splits the trunk of a many-ringed tree right down the middle. it's not strange to think we can still save each other. your voice, that shrillness, like a wire pulled through teeth. somedays, I chew to prove I can still bite.
