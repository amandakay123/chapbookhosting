In the Mood for Love Poem

After Wong Kar-wai

wan-chai and again I am dreaming of your lips, pursing as you speak. english the language greasing the insides of our cheeks, the cleaver raised and felled. a goose neck wrung, twin t-shirts on the clothesline. it's summer, but a damp one. like the waxy film ice cream leaves on your tongue. we had shared a cone from mobile softee's down at the harbour. so many things I tell you then. hong kong no longer a city of neon, maintenance codes revising history, how easily 120000 become 500. LEDs our new illumination. displacement a fact of water and people. I imagine a meniscus wobbling, staring at the contours of your face, hair like corn silk, only familiar in gender. the sweat on your shirt dripped onto the sidewalk, distorting.

summer, 2024. your nails back then, painted the vibrant green of dyed jade. how you held me with the knowledge of something fragile in your hands, like playing cards or bone-china, all things sharpened to a point. a black kite flew overhead, strangling a squirrel in its grasp. how gentle, your hands then, around my throat. had I tried to breathe then maybe I would've realized how ineffective the diaphragm is at collecting. coins clank, fumble around in your pocket.

truth is I get flashbacks just seeing your fingernails clipped. I pack my sandwiches into plastic bags, layer the bread and the lettuce and the cheese, worry the insides of my cheeks into a tomato's red. some eurasian deer staggering in the bramble.

in the months that I knew you we'd hop on random buses and get off at random stops, to look every stranger in the eye. I wanted to not just be seen, but to be seen with you, so the soles of our rubber shoes gripping cigarette butts grind themselves into tire threads set alight embers or woodchips, more of the same when turned to exhaust. creatures of bioluminescence, our bodies laid awash beneath the signs. spoonbills entangle themselves midair. let it be the clothesline or the telephone wires. their wings strung with twine, sun baking skin like roasted goose. the movement of its feathers rotoscoped as if practiced or drawn together. morsels of fat dripping, in light, amber or motor oil all the same.

one saturday night and takeout, ordering for two. that night you told me you dreamt of my suicide. that night only one plate of food was touched. the intimacy of fingertips against soda tab.

english still language cutting the inside of my throat. my most masculine trait: to go out in a blaze of glory. the body keeps the score. fifteen points to whoever guesses the next way I try to leave! you say you hate the brash way I move through the world but when the time comes, will we not end with the swashbucklers and the creditors? I was making good on all my deals. sweating all debts from my blood.

on that avenue of stars I remember the way your palm slotted into mine. the roughness of your hands like bark, of roots sapping water. it was the fluid from my spine. I could no longer hold myself up nor breathe. I am still thinking about your lips.

there's a scene in every movie where the main character walks past their younger self, changed, partly blessed with knowledge, but more so cursed with eyes made hollow and the tatter of clothes, like a flag. when we met, I saw you in this previous self. your painted nails forming half-moons on my inner thigh. the moon narrowing at the mouth of the delta, carrying with it glow-in-the-dark algae and six american flags.

some days, leaving you in the space between convenience stores and wet markets. some days, the lakes drain themselves of color some days, lymphatic drainage, GLP-1s someday, hopefully soon after The Strokes

there's oil in the rivers and it's choking the flowers! yellow on the concrete, call it piss or marigolds. oh all my poor marigolds, drowned. beget new rivers to cross! grocery shopping in a time of crisis: inflation, some strange kink or the expansion of the world. eastern, in fact, towards the sunrise.

those days, my lunch was a buy-one-get-one free rice ball from the 7-11 beneath my mong kok apartment, they were filled with milky yolk or tuna mayo or mentaiko. if I was good my lunch would be my dinner. I drank water to quench the hunger. opened my mouth wide at every bus stop. upon querying chat-gpt, he too, shared thirst.

what of the spoonbills? you asked. the spoonbills shitting gold, those spoonbills shot dead. I'll swallow anything to feel full. the microplastics of the heart, lapsing into bloodstream. what of capillary action? only so much sky to go around.

let me tell you this secret. I am telling you this secret, mouthed by my lips, pressed into wall, sealed with mud. after wong kar wai, in the mood for love. this secret, you had yelled from the top of victoria peak, but the summer wind caught your words so I could not hear you, compensated with your lips against my ear all night long. instead heat, that brush-fire, all the way back home, that for me, was heard in california, where the calla-lilies strangle the land's native grasses, where our tongues met but only through the chorus of so many other voices, the air perturbed by light or by crimson, had the berry not been crushed underfoot nor the cigarette been lit nor the cruel undergrowth choked.

how one saturday night, you tell me you are "in the mood for love." oh love, what was it called when the neon filtered through the window, casting its sticky film? technicolor awash over the dirty sheets. keeping all the lights on. vision a necessary expenditure. the heart stretches like clingfilm but not nearly enough. at night I cross an ocean of my own sweat, marooning language onto a rocky shore. crying out in the dark. the language shipwrecks back. the imprint of my forehead stains your pillow. you no longer want to be here.

still light, some gentle sliver, dapples. the hide of a fawn, stumbling drunk naked. my most feminine trait. I want to be thin, if not beautiful. ideally both. and hairless to add! skinny like the straight line of a riding crop. I cannot tell if I want the mare or the stallion in my blood so I settle for the whip- lash. when you had moved from new york, the plane almost jolted out of the sky, made love to the earth. the swoosh of air against aluminium. the sky some strange park for your amusement. we makeout in front of the headlights. red ochre peels away the asphalt's false skin. earth's pockmarked chest exhales.

my head shifts on your lap. strange, because despite all this memory, I refuse to recall your face. smog floats over the water. the light glints off your hair, catches in the tendrils. I imagine, had there been a last shot of this movie, it would have already turned to black. our bodies were neon and enacted in technicolor, oh what the laws demand the discard the lack. doesn't matter the days like cellophane. the self-portrait in the kitchen grease. leaving lipstick on the glass. making small graves in the bramble. 1980s, back when the city was neon, there were only 300 black-faced spoonbills left in the wild. every alleyway exuded light. extinction a matter of feathers.

down with the love poems the cinema the masquerades of and with faith. down with AI generated horoscopes and the sound of the wind moving through the deergrass. down with masks and mases and mass, whether in weight or worship. down with the neon. the tides enshroud the dusk, swallows the spoonbill's cries. we search for nobler gases.

A—, do you remember the last time you were in love?

dawn crests over the tenements. a black kite carries a burning branch, vows to flush out its prey. voles tremble in the underbrush. the kite's hollow turns to ash. I burrow against all light.
