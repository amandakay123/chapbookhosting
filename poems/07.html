honeysuckle

at the teat of some new dawn. summer rolls against the underside of your tongue, softens into silk. morning, and again, we wake and disrobe. doubts skimmed, the surface of lemon curd. how quickly the surface tension breaks. I picture time as the flour that sifts through your hands, some new love sticking to the fold in your shirt. from here, even an eyelash can be the petal captured in mid-flight, then rotting. together, we pluck the crimson, the magenta, then bruise. some fragrance emerges from the emulsion, turns the cloying breath of an autumn rain. there's nothing notable about leaving. again and again, new ground replaces us. a crow, drawn to carrion, now summoned. what's left of our sourness clings to beak, stifles its call.
