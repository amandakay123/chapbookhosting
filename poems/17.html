in my friend's sf apartment, post-rain

for J

cradling mugs of ginger tea. steam rising, then dissipating. it has been a long time since we had last smoked together. longer still from the times we'd walk on our college campus, night ushering in the crows and the owls, that nostalgic winter breeze enveloping our hands. walking through the world hurt but less so, still naive to the particularities of grief, for how those accumulations will show on our faces. oh J—, I say, how much can change in a year. truth that I no longer recognize the people I'm with nor the dog's faces in the grass. the house we had lived in together bearing new weight, creaks in time with the living room's clock. you tell me about your city's strange rattles and its sycophantic beasts, those tall san francisco billboards. everything worth dying for: all-caps, bold font. I tell you about the men I loved and the men I'd like to love, feelings carried course and swift on a raven's wing. and underneath that dim lighting I tell you every time I've been splayed open, heart beating towards the sky, the moon the pearly sheen of my ribcage, twisted apart. I take your advice; we speak about sharpening our skin against the world. treating our bodies as turnstiles permitting entry to kind, beautiful strangers. scanning the perimeter for worrying signs. still looking for movement in the deergrass. still hearing the dawn fill with animal sounds, the coyote's cries. I do not want the world to make me unkind. to demand to know who leaves and who enters. that is not the lesson of this story. when I was young, to feel pain meant I was alive. today I put pressure on every old scar, every lost-love refracting in the dying light. the dregs of saturday drip in rivulets down the window. on your couch I read a poem, then set the book down. stared at the ceiling for minutes, if not hours. dear world, let me spend more time with people who will hurt me. to burn my tongue on more cups of tea. if only just to taste everything this world has to offer, open-mouthed.
