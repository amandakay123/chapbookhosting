Future Mourning

To T

I, being a superstitious child, avoided cracks in the road. checked thrice that I turned the stove off before heading upstairs. turned the faucet on and off until I could be sure it was fully closed. spent hours and hours dreaming of better endings, avoided mentions of death until it looked me straight in the eyes, only facing my reflection in the glass when it was close enough to touch. but you father, were different. you treated Death to a staring contest that you knew were losing. invited him to family dinners, his fingerprints on every fast-food wrapper and #1 Best Dad mug I gifted you. you make another joke about saving for your funeral (to cremate you instead, and spread you all over the porch) and suddenly my footsteps marching down the steps are the same that will carry me to the front of the church on that day. which is to say, dad, one day I will wake up to my children asking me about their grandfather and all you will be is beautiful grass. one day, my son will grow into your face and all I'll feel is nostalgia and the cosmic death of the sun. I will not have the vocabulary to describe your life but I will still pray for the birds to sing at your funeral, the body I loved encased in mahogany, and soon after the short montage of your leaving I will think of your hands, the last time your calluses met their pair in mine, everything good dying in California and the rough rings of a felled Redwood in your grip. I will pray for the birds to take me home, to the house you raised me in, and look for you in every picture I pass, but will not find you, no matter how many rooms I haunt, because you are not there, and all I will have is missing you and grass so thick that I could drown in it, your loneliness in your final days becoming mine, where we are two parallel lines but one has stopped moving forward.
