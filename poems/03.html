Poem From the Center of the Room

so easy to mistake veins as the movement of electrons, the tremble of telephone wires. leave me the landline gripping the ground, its fangs growing rust. leave me the memory of a mother, the body of the obsolete. leave me & trace your hands over the smallest of corners, everywhere we'd laid shadow like brick, felt the soft darkness ooze through our fingers. which is to say, I'm now realizing how your presence inverts the white space. disrupts the air like cargo plane through forest, to surrender like gravity. a whistle breaks the cries, pierces the sound barrier. teaches limbs flight, lungs the drowning. it's so easy to imagine our life as a house fire, a plane crash, an earthquake unrooting. your mother, who contemplates divorce on the anniversary of your dog's death, forgets to visit. dedicated to this nation of empty supermarket aisles. love, the ground is still wet. your hair catches the light. my brother calls with bad news. still, the curtain stays drawn. some measured breath opens, then shudders closed.
