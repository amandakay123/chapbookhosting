Love Poem As Seen Through the Fiber Optic

I'm telling you that I'll go outside tomorrow. but for now this bed is so comfy and this shower is so warm and my back no longer aches with little droplets of rain, all discard from the sky. instead I thank the inventor of the portable heater in my small dorm room as I watch the news, CNN, scroll through TikTok. I sleep then wake up thirsty, sweat beading on my forehead, dissipating on pillow. how the hours, not moving through me but turning like the treads on an elliptical, extend into the infinite. what a horizon to chase. it's so easy to quantify life when captured on the LED, made beautiful in green or red or yellow. I'm in love with the little beeps, the microwave's slow rotation, another message going ignored. hanging up the phone and saying that, in my defense, I don't do well with the unexpected. who knew that twenty-one was the age when your friends start dying, smeared across an eight-lane highway or accidentally found at the bottom of a pool or something like it all happened so fast, wearing his fugly cat-print socks. didn't have the chance to make the bed nor close every damning tab. but, no, people have always been dying, not just at twenty-one, but I like to think that it begins now, that my act of self-isolation is in response to death's new phenomenon, instinct becoming the animal burrowing itself deeper into the muck. seeing life as a series of accidents we avoid until they make themselves unavoidable. okay, maybe that's unfair. a lot of life isn't accidents and a lot of death isn't either. you were meticulous, planned to the T. you made sure it didn't hurt you or me either, which turned out to be a lie in both parts. still, I'll tell this story because it's the story you've written and I'm nothing if not consistent. struck at how even the branches outside my window fail to mimic your finger's trembling. which is to say, I want to hold your hand again. I summon you in this lossless room, refracted onto my bedroom walls, little particles of life holding just moments of your face. look, there was none of you to start with and now there are several, maybe even five! these pixels rearranging themselves in perfect slideshow. it's no wonder why to reminisce is to see you, all over my room, in the faces cloud make before contorting back into fluff, in the baby hornworm that buries itself deep into a strawberry's flesh. if you've ever loved me don't let me know, not when you are the shadows in the leaves, the breeze in my hair. a heron settles on the lake, its feathers laid slick in a row. dawn rattles the window frame. I could've taken a picture of the moment before it chose flight, but I'm telling you that I'll go outside tomorrow, still waiting for a clear day.
