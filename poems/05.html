Poem on a Bus to Big Sur (Late May, 2026)

strange to think about how the way time learns to escape, walking past my freshman year dorm or feeling the rubber treads of a big tour bus peel away from campus. talking about jumping off Bixby Creek Bridge. telling myself the world hurts you no matter what so make it beautiful, imagining taking flight from the lone conifer where seabirds roost as they drag their tired wings back to shore. how the water grays itself into one straight line past the horizon. the light, perturbed by a brewing storm, makes mourning seem futile, so close is darkness to everything cerulean and serene. but from where we stand in the sand, the water is so blue and erosion has not acted upon us yet. instead the waves crashing onto rocks sing us a lullaby. of light turning dappled through the spindly branches of a Monterey pine. of this spring's wildflowers. learning how to bloom, learning to embrace their first gust of wind, to dig their roots deep into the soil, letting each individual petal fall as they may. let the days I've spent asleep wash away with the river's wishes. let me startle awake from the cold. some brave seed floats on its surface, not yet sure of where it's going, not really sure of where it came from in the first place. all it knows is how to burgeon out of the thick confines of its shell, shredding layers of hair and husk, coming to rest in some foreign patch of green where there is no other choice but to be bathed in the morning dew and the dawn's first light.
