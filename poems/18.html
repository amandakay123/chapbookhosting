on the kairos front lawn

eating an assortment of fruits in the midday sun, grass like pinpricks through the cotton of my shirt. heat swelling on the skin of my face, still bare. how the vesicles of a blood orange, dark red and plump, bursted bitter. let me tell you what else lingered on tongue. for weeks I dreaded whatever came next, ran from light, some rat scurrying into its rat-hole scraping the pads of its paws on the cold tarmac. bracing itself against clever mousetraps, I thought that winter would make me kinder to myself, let the hoarfrost linger on the tips of my eyelashes, cloud my vision from the darkening sky. instead, it made me plump and unattractive, too red on the inside and the out, everything bursting out of me. the sky fills with sirens. bodies return to the ground. the music will not come home. but on the breeze today, I hear a robin's chirps. lizards move through the rocks. they are guided, partly by instinct, partly by heat. after the first day of good sun, I spent an entire day walking. my feet in tune to the rush of water through the reeds. I can tell that I still miss you but the universe, hinging on its absences, rearranges its atoms. I avoid known paths. hearing the same sound, different river. how delicate the tree-shadow on its banks. despite everything, I still want to be beautiful. to lay in the midday sun, casting my heart, my shadow, to any passerbyer looking my way.
