Letter to My Mother on the Florence-Bound Train

Strange to see the places you've dreamed of, some bookmarked page, made tangible in camera shutter, steel rattle. Your oldest daughter's skin, bronzing beneath a foreign sun. This, a language neither of us speaks: the sound of sand shifting under a tourist's feet. How we too, begin to erode. In the last photo I texted you, I was at a beach somewhere, caught between Vernazza's rocky coast and houses the color of candy, paled by salt. My face, turned from the flash. You imagine this expression. Behind me, the mountains bear soft fruit: a vineyard of splintered fences, the sea's glittering light. Allora, this is how we begin: a path leaving the sandbox, winding down to the base of some small town we eventually root in. What words had we buried there, muffled by some gentle rumble, a gunshot sky, steam escaping the kettle's aluminum chamber. A cloud in the shape of a whale, migrating from childhood's ceilings. You taught me the many ways to slip onto these rain-slick tracks, their god-hummed silence. The pupil now a fallen seed, flowering wherever it lands. We don't leave the body, only the memory of where it has been. Mistaking synonym for symptom, I call my psychiatrist on Zoom. Your name in the thesaurus next to fireflies. The landline thrums, giving electricity to this nightfall. It is so easy to be struck by the familiar: the scent of your perfume, lifted on a stranger's wind. Some farmer's calf runs across the road, this spring's mud soft on its hooves. It does not yet know to fear the headlights, still so scared of the dark.
